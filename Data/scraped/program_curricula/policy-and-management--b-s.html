Overview

Great organizations do not succeed by accident. They depend on policymakers, managers, and other leaders who can bring together people, evidence, and strategy to solve difficult problems. The Policy and Management major prepares students for exactly those roles by combining scientific analysis, strategic thinking, negotiation, and policy expertise, equipping them to address complex challenges facing businesses, governments, nonprofits, healthcare systems, and other organizations.
 
Students learn how to develop effective strategies, negotiate among competing interests, evaluate evidence, communicate across multiple stakeholders, and lead organizations through uncertainty and change. They learn why good ideas often fail in practice and how effective leaders translate evidence into action while balancing economic, political, and organizational realities.
 
Rigorous scientific and analytical training is paired with extensive opportunities to apply these ideas to real-world problems. Students develop expertise in research design, data analysis, decision analysis, negotiation, and policy evaluation while working closely with faculty on hands-on projects. The major culminates in a capstone project in which students tackle an important organizational or policy challenge, integrating the analytical, behavioral, and leadership skills they have developed throughout the program.
 
Organizations increasingly seek leaders who can combine analytical thinking with an understanding of people, incentives, and institutions. Graduates are well prepared for careers in management, consulting, strategy, public policy, government, nonprofit leadership, analytics, and many other fields, as well as graduate and professional study in business, public policy, law, economics, and related disciplines.

This is presented as a recommended plan for completing major requirements.  Students may declare their major as early as the third week of the spring semester in the first year. Students who are planning to attend the Washington Semester Program, to study abroad, to apply for the Heinz Accelerated Masters Program, or to pursue an additional major/minor may have a very different curriculum map and should consult early – and often – with the Policy and Management Academic Advisor.

Students are encouraged to consider the Washington Semester Program as part of their education. Suitable courses may be considered as fulfilling requirements of concentration electives in the major.  Please discuss course selections with the Academic Advisor during the application phase to the program. 

Additional Major

Students who elect Policy and Management as an additional major must fulfill all of the requirements of the Policy and Management major.  For additional majors in Policy and Management, courses taken as concentration electives may not count toward the student's primary major or other program.

Students pursuing Behavioral Economics, Policy, and Organizations with an additional major in Policy and Management may only count 36-202 , 73-102, and 88-251 (and 88-252, if taken in Empirical Core) toward the completion of both majors.

Students pursuing Decision Science with an additional major in Policy and Management may only count 36-202 , 73-102, 88-223 , and 88-251 toward the completion of both majors.

Additional majors cannot count menu electives toward simultaneously fulfilling more than one major or minor. Students who are interested in an additional major in Policy and Management should see the Academic Advisor of the Policy and Management program.
